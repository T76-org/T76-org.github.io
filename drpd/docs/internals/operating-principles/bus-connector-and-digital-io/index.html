Bus Connector and Digital IO

This section of the schematic is responsible for handling the interconnection between the two CC lines on the USB-C ports, as well as all digital USB-PD communications.

Cable orientation detection​

Even though USB-C cables feature two CC lines, only one of them is used for communication at a time; the other is either left floating or used to power the cable's e-marker.

Thus, when a cable is connected to Port 1 and the device is in Sink mode, it needs to figure out which CC line is actually usable for communication. In Observer mode, the device needs to figure out which CC line is being used by either port, and then connect them accordingly so that the attached devices can communicate with each other.

To accomplish this dance, Dr. PD uses three small 2:1 multiplexers, U503BOMSchematic, U504BOMSchematic, and U505BOMSchematic. Each of these multiplexers has two inputs and one output, and can be configured to connect either input to the output:

  • U503BOMSchematic is capable of redirecting either CC line from Port 1 on to one of the inputs of U504BOMSchematic.
  • U505BOMSchematic is likewise capable of redirecting either CC line from Port 2 on to the other input of U504BOMSchematic.
  • U504BOMSchematic is then responsible for either connecting the two selected CC lines together or leaving them isolated from each other.

The cable orientation mechanism works in slightly different ways depending on whether the device is in Sink mode or Observer mode.

Sink mode​

In Sink mode, U504BOMSchematic is always configured to isolate the two CC lines from each other, since only Port 1 is used for communication. This prevents a device or cable connected to Port 2 from interfering with the signaling between Port 1 and Dr. PD's own internal transmitter.

When Dr. PD enters Sink mode, the PD Role Resistors circuit first places the appropriate pull-down resistor on both CC lines of Port 1. The Analog Monitor then measures both lines. If a source is connected, its pull-up resistor and Dr. PD's pull-down resistor establish a characteristic voltage on the active CC line. The firmware currently recognizes an attached source when the measured CC voltage is greater than 0.75 V and less than 2.0 V, then debounces the detection before selecting that line for communication. The firmware then configures U503BOMSchematic to route that line to Dr. PD's receiver and transmitter. If the source is working correctly, it starts sending PD messages that can then be processed by the firmware.

Observer mode​

In Observer mode, U504BOMSchematic is initially configured to isolate the two ports. The firmware activates a state machine that examines all four CC lines for one whose voltage exceeds the configured 0.75 V source-detection threshold. After debouncing the detection, it records that line and port as the source candidate, then cycles through the two CC lines of the other port, connecting them through U504BOMSchematic to the selected CC line of the source port in turn until it finds one that pulls down the line to a voltage consistent with the presence of a sink device's 5.1kΩ pull-down resistor. Once it finds a valid CC line on the sink port, it locks that as the port choice for the sink, and leaves U504BOMSchematic configured to connect the two selected CC lines together.

The process has several failure modes, which the firmware handles by backing the state machine off of its port assignments as needed. This may be necessary, for example, if one or both of the devices attached to the USB-C ports are dual-role devices, in which case they alternate their connectivity between source and sink mode, and several attach cycles may be required until both devices align in a compatible way.

info

Note that this is a non-standard process that is not part of the USB-PD specification, since the specification assumes that the two devices are connected directly to each other, and thus can communicate with each other directly.

Dr. PD defeats this assumption by interposing itself between the two devices and requiring two cables, which may be of different lengths and orientations, and thus may not be able to communicate with each other directly.

CC edge detector​

U502ABOMSchematic converts the analog CC waveform into the digital CC_IN signal by comparing it with a programmable reference voltage. Its output changes state on both upward and downward threshold crossings. The firmware treats the first low-to-high transition after an idle period as the start of a frame, then measures the time between successive falling and rising transitions to decode the BMC signal. The reference is created by feeding a PWM signal from the firmware into R508BOMSchematic, R509BOMSchematic, R510BOMSchematic, C503BOMSchematic, C504BOMSchematic, and C506BOMSchematic, which, together with U501ABOMSchematic, form a second-order active low-pass filter.

By default, the reference voltage is set to 0.7 V, which has proven to work with the vast majority of the USB-C devices that we have tested. It can be adjusted through Device → Calibrate → Internal settings… in the frontend's BMC Decoder configuration or by using the corresponding SCPI command.

CC transmission circuit​

The last bit of functionality contained in this section of the schematic is used by the firmware when it needs to transmit data on the CC lines. R501BOMSchematic, R502BOMSchematic, and R503BOMSchematic form a simple voltage divider that generates a reference voltage of about 1.1V. This, in turn, is buffered by U501BBOMSchematic, providing a stiff voltage source that represents the “high” state of the CC line, as required by the USB-PD specification.

The transmitter uses separate CC_OUT and CC_OUT_EN signals. During transmission, CC_OUT carries the BMC waveform while CC_OUT_EN enables the analog output stage. Between messages, the firmware deasserts CC_OUT_EN, placing the transmitter in a high-impedance state so that it does not disturb the CC line's normal bias voltage.

CC_OUT controls two complementary current-mirror branches. When CC_OUT is low, the PNP pair Q502BOMSchematic and Q505BOMSchematic sources current from the buffered 1.1 V reference into the transmitter output. When CC_OUT is high, the NPN pair Q504BOMSchematic and Q503BOMSchematic sinks current from the output to ground. R14BOMSchematic, R504BOMSchematic, and R505BOMSchematic establish the reference currents. By charging and discharging the CC-line capacitance with controlled currents, the mirrors limit the waveform's slew rate, reducing high-frequency energy, ringing, and EMI while producing edges compatible with the USB-PD physical-layer requirements.

There is one more wrinkle to deal with: The CC lines are normally held at a voltage between approximately 0.9V and 1.5V by the combination of the source's pull-up resistor and the sink's pull-down resistor. Because the transmission current mirrors are fairly low impedance, this means that they would normally tend to pull down the line voltage below the normal range, which might inhibit communication on the line or even be interpreted as a disconnection event.

Q501BOMSchematic connects the current-mirror output to the selected CC line only while transmission is enabled. R506BOMSchematic pulls Q501BOMSchematic's gate to ground by default, keeping it off and isolating the transmitter. When the firmware asserts CC_OUT_EN, the signal drives Q501BOMSchematic's gate through R16BOMSchematic, turning it on and allowing the sourcing and sinking branches to drive the CC line. Deasserting CC_OUT_EN turns Q501BOMSchematic off and returns the transmitter to its high-impedance state.

note

Q501BOMSchematic may appear backwards compared with a typical N-channel MOSFET switch, but its orientation is deliberate: when Q501BOMSchematic is off, its body diode is oriented to prevent current from entering the output stage from the CC line. When Q501BOMSchematic is on, its channel allows the transmitter to both source and sink current.
