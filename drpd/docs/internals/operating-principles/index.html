Operating principles

In this section, we describe how Dr. PD's hardware is designed, with a focus on the principles that guide its development and operation.

Basic philosophy​

We wanted Dr. PD to be easy to use and reliable, but also open to experimentation and modification. To achieve this, we adopted a few key principles:

  • No protocol black boxes: We implemented the USB-PD stack in firmware rather than relying on a dedicated USB-PD controller chip. The supporting CC, measurement, and power-control circuits use discrete or general-purpose components whose behavior can be understood from the schematic and their datasheets. This makes it easier to inspect, modify, and extend Dr. PD without being limited by the fixed capabilities of a dedicated controller.
  • Jellybean components: We've used common components that are easy to source and replace from multiple vendors. Dr. PD is built almost entirely from standard parts. Where a function depends on a single-source part—most notably the RP2354 microcontroller—we favor devices that are widely available, feature open-source tooling, and are supported by a large community of developers.
  • Ease of repair: Where practical, we've used large SMD packages—typically 1206 for passive components—and avoided packages that are difficult to solder or replace when suitable alternatives are available. To make troubleshooting easier, we also placed components on a widely-spaced grid and added plenty of test points for critical signals.

Basic architecture​

If you look at the schematic, you'll see that Dr. PD is built around 5 main functional blocks:

  • USB Ports (2xx-series components) defines the front-panel USB-C connectors, as well as the associated power switching and sensing circuitry.
  • The Analog Monitor (3xx-series components) is responsible for measuring critical voltages and currents on VBUS and various signal lines.
  • The PD Role Resistors (4xx-series components) contain two multiplexed sets of resistors that are used to configure the USB-PD role of the device in Sink mode (as well as a possible future Source mode). These resistors are switched in and out of the circuit by a pair of analog switches, which are controlled by the microcontroller.
  • The Bus Connector and Digital IO (5xx-series components) is responsible for detecting the orientation of the USB-C cables in both front-panel ports, capturing CC line edges, and actively driving the CC output in Sink mode. It also provides a digital interface to the microcontroller for controlling the analog switches and reading the analog monitor.
  • Finally, the Digital Control (6xx-series components) section contains the microcontroller and its associated circuitry, including internal power rails and voltage references.

To make it a bit easier to follow the logical layout of the system, 1xx-series components are all located in the main schematic sheet, while 2xx-6xx-series components are located in a separate sub-sheet for each functional block.

Protection mechanisms​

Dr. PD implements ESD protection on all exposed lines, including the front- and back-panel USB-C connectors, the CC OUTPUT port, and the SYNC port.

D101BOMSchematic and D102BOMSchematic are TVS diodes that protect the CC OUT and SYNC ports. The CC lines on ports 1 and 2 are similarly protected by D501BOMSchematic, while D601BOMSchematic protects the USB 2.0 data and VBUS lines on the Host USB-C port.

D301BOMSchematic is a 60 V stand-off TVS diode that provides a discharge path for transient overvoltage events on the VBUS line.

In addition to ESD protection, Dr. PD also implements reverse-polarity protection on the VBUS line; the P-channel MOSFET Q101BOMSchematic is used to block current flow if the VBUS line is connected with the wrong polarity. Zener diode D103BOMSchematic limits the gate-to-source voltage of Q101BOMSchematic to around 12V, preventing damage to the MOSFET when the voltage on VBUS exceeds the maximum gate-to-source voltage of 20V, with extra headroom for safety. R103BOMSchematic pulls Q101BOMSchematic's gate toward ground. With correctly polarized VBUS, this produces a negative gate-to-source voltage that turns Q101BOMSchematic on. If the banana connectors are reverse-polarized, Q101BOMSchematic remains off and blocks current through the VBUS path.

Finally, R101BOMSchematic and R102BOMSchematic are used to limit current on the SYNC port. At a total of 20Ω, these resistors offer only limited protection, but this compromise is necessary because the port can be used to both drive a load and sink current from a pull-up resistor on the other end, and so it would be undesirable to increase their value further.

Additional protection is provided by bidirectional switches on both Port 1 and Port 2; these are controlled by the microcontroller and are used to disconnect ports when they are not in use and to implement Dr. PD's programmable overvoltage and overcurrent protection.
