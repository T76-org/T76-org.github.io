Front and back panels

Dr. PD separates the USB-PD connection under test from its host connection and bench interfaces. The front panel carries the two USB-C test ports, VBUS access, and the CC monitoring output. The back panel carries the host USB connection and the configurable sync output.

Front panel​

Port 1 — Sink Mode DUT​

Port 1 is the primary USB-C port for the device under test. Connect a USB-PD source here when using Dr. PD in Sink mode. Dr. PD can then discover the source's capabilities, request a supported power contract, capture the USB-PD exchange, and measure VBUS voltage and current.

Port 1 is also one side of the pass-through connection in Observer mode.

warning

Before testing high-power or unfamiliar devices, read the important warnings and safety precautions.

Port 2 — USB-C pass-through​

Port 2 is the other side of the USB-C pass-through path. In Observer mode, connect Dr. PD inline between a source and a sink using Ports 1 and 2. Dr. PD then observes their USB-PD communication and measures the power flowing through the connection.

tip

In Observer mode, both ports are bidirectional, so the source and sink can be connected to either port. They can also swap power roles while remaining connected (as required by the USB-PD spec).

The power diagram in the control software will show the direction in which power is flowing, and the Message Log will show which device is currently acting as the source and which is acting as the sink.

In Sink mode, Port 2 is disconnected from the USB-PD bus and VBUS line. Even with that lockout, it's a good idea to avoid connecting a USB-PD source to Port 2 to prevent accidental power delivery to it.

VBUS banana jacks​

The positive and negative banana jacks provide access to VBUS. In Sink mode, use them to connect an external electronic load, a meter, or other suitably rated bench equipment to the negotiated supply. In Observer mode, use them to measure the voltage and current flowing through the USB-C connection.

important

Always observe the polarity and the limits printed on the panel: 60 V maximum and 6 A maximum at the connectors. Dr. PD's specified VBUS measurement range is 0–60 V and ±6 A; the supported USB-PD sink request is also limited by the source, cable, negotiated contract, and applicable power rating. Configure OVP and OCP before connecting an unfamiliar source or load.

CC OUT​

CC OUT is a dedicated monitoring tap for the active USB-C Configuration Channel signal. Connect it to suitably rated oscilloscope or logic-analyzer equipment when you want to compare the physical CC waveform with decoded messages or the SYNC output.

tip

Dr. PD automatically accounts for cable orientation and exposes the correct CC connection on both ports. Therefore, the CC OUT signal always corresponds to the active CC line on the USB-C connection under test.

Back panel​

SYNC​

SYNC is the configurable hardware trigger output. Connect it to an oscilloscope, logic analyzer, or other compatible instrument to correlate an external capture with events such as packet starts, message completion, hard resets, or protocol errors.

The SYNC connector is limited to 3.3 V and 5 mA. Its pulse behavior, event, threshold, and repeat settings are configured in the control software. See Using triggers and the sync output for setup and timing considerations.

HOST​

The HOST USB-C port powers Dr. PD and connects it to the control computer. Use a USB-C cable that supports USB 2.0 data, not a charge-only cable. Open the Dr. PD control software in Chrome or Edge before connecting the instrument, then pair it when prompted.

The host connection is separate from Ports 1 and 2: attach the computer here, and reserve the front USB-C ports for the USB-PD connection under test.
